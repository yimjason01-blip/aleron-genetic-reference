01Every page reads in three steps

  1. Result. Pick the result printed on the report. It decides which risk table and which guide apply, so it comes first.
  2. Risk table. The patient's absolute risk next to an ordinary person's, for the three conditions that matter most.
  3. Guidance. Invitae's own guide for that result, linked. We do not write management advice.

02Step 1: the result

A gene offers the results Invitae writes a guide for, plus the carrier result for recessive genes. Most genes have one result and show it as a fixed line; a switch appears only when there is a real choice. Each option is named in report terms, with the report's wording under it.

Heterozygous
One variant, one copy affected. For a recessive gene this is a carrier: personal risk is usually normal, and the finding matters for family planning.
Biallelic
Both copies affected. The report shows it as homozygous, or as two different heterozygous variants. These share one option because they share the same risk and guide. Two different variants count only once shown to sit on opposite copies.
X-linked
One result line, hemizygous in men and heterozygous in women. The table's Women and Men columns already carry the difference, so there is no sex switch.
Carrier
For a recessive gene, the heterozygous result shows no risk table, only what it means for the patient and their children.
Specific variant
A named variant with its own published risk, such as ATM c.7271T>G, is a second choice under its result. It replaces only the table rows its evidence covers. Where no absolute risk is published, it says so instead of showing a table.

Under the switch: one or two sentences on what the result means, and a check only where it prevents a wrong action. Everything else goes below the table. The table heading names the selected result. A result appears only when it can occur and the gene says something different for it.

03Step 2: the risk table

The table answers one question: what risk does this patient face, against an ordinary person of the same sex? Absolute risk comes first because it is what changes a decision; a ratio alone can make a small risk look large.

  • Columns. Women and Men, always separate. Each has Absolute risk, Baseline risk (same outcome, sex and age span in the general population) and Risk comparison (absolute divided by baseline; "about" when the two come from different studies).
  • Under every figure, one line naming the age span and who was studied, for example by age 80 years · carrier families.
  • At most three rows. Serious conditions first (can kill, cause organ failure or permanent loss of function). Within those, the largest excess risk first. Everything else stays on the page below the table, nothing is deleted.
  • Rows never used: composites such as "any cancer", the syndrome itself, outcomes that only follow the diagnosis, and figures resting on a handful of patients.
Not established
No published figure.
Not comparable
Both figures exist but cannot be divided.
Not applicable
The sex cannot have the condition, or it only occurs with the variant.

04Step 3: guidance

Guidance links Labcorp's Invitae positive results guide that matches the selected result. Invitae attaches these guides to the report and keeps them current, so rewriting them would only create a second version to defend. Recessive genes with no carrier guide show one line on what a carrier result means.

05Where the numbers come from

  • Patient figures, best first: studies that test everyone and follow who gets sick, tested relatives, family or clinic cohorts, then frequencies in diagnosed patients as a last resort. A table resting only on diagnosed patients says the screened patient's risk is likely lower.
  • Baselines: one figure per condition and sex, shared by every gene. Cancers use SEER. Others use a named registry or population study.
  • Every figure links its study. Figures found by literature search also record the quoted sentence.

06Open items

  • No clinician has reviewed any figure. About 650 came from a literature search and have not been re-checked against each paper.
  • The result meanings were condensed from each gene's record and checked gene by gene against it (103 findings, 93 fixed; log in an internal log). No clinician has reviewed them.
  • Specific-variant figures confirmed against the source text: ATM c.7271T>G, BRCA1 c.5096G>A, TP53 c.542G>A, HOXB13 G84E. APC I1307K shows no figure: no published absolute risk could be verified.
  • The list of serious conditions is a judgment call. Changing it re-ranks every table.
